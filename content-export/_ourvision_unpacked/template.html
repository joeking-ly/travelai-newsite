Our Vision

One vision, written down as we go.

We've been building toward the same future for years: travel that knows you, works for you, and remembers you without taking you away from the trip itself. Along the way we've written down what we saw coming, at the moment we saw it. Each paper below is a marker in time. Read together, they trace a single line toward the four things the agentic age requires, and what we're doing about each one.

01 / 04
01

Memory

Agents are amnesiacs. Travelers pay for it.

Agents are only as good as the context they hold, and today every agent is an amnesiac. Traveler.md and the Traveler Action Model make travel context portable, human readable, and owned by the traveler. Granted to any agent. Revoked anytime.

02

Governance

No enterprise puts agents to work ungoverned.

Consent, audit, and replay machinery lets OTAs, TMCs, and enterprise travel programs adopt traveler memory with every interaction traceable and every decision defensible. That's the Governed Memory Platform.

03

Trust

Memory only moves when everyone can see it move.

Memory only moves if every participant can see what's shared, with whose permission, and to what end. The Shared Vector Travel Protocol moves traveler context between partners without exposing the person behind it.

04

Distribution

None of it matters without real travel.

None of it matters without real travelers, real bookings, and real supply to run on. Our network of 530+ travel brands reaches 50M+ travelers a year and generates $630M+ in bookings. It is not a legacy business that funds the vision. It is the distribution layer the vision requires, and the proving ground where the other three pillars meet production traffic every day.

The agent layer will be competitive and fast moving. The layer underneath, where these four requirements live, should be cooperative, stable, and the traveler's. That's the layer we're building. The papers below trace how each requirement came into focus.

Building Our Factory of the Future

Before there was a memory layer, there had to be a machine that could learn. This paper describes the operating philosophy behind TravelAI: a virtual factory that builds the thing that builds the thing. Automate the 80% of work every business shares, so our people, and now our agents, can pour themselves into the 20% that's ours alone. The point was never automation for its own sake. It was to build an engine efficient enough to make travel better for a billion people, and human enough to keep its soul. That engine is what turned a portfolio of brands into a distribution layer.

"Our job, and humanity's job, is to infuse the soul into the machine."

The AI Power Shift Trust, the shift

The Third Voice

If the factory was about how we build, the Third Voice is about what's changing in the market we build for. Travel has always been a two sided marketplace: travelers and suppliers. AI introduces a third participant: agents that don't just assist, but decide, negotiate, and act. We argued that whoever holds this Third Voice will shape the customer experience for the next decade, and that it must be aligned to the interests travelers and suppliers already share. That alignment question is a trust question, and it's why the protocol layer matters as much as the agents themselves.

"Soon, you won't just search for travel. Travel will come to you."

The portable memory layer Memory, the traveler

Traveler.md

Capable agents are here. But every one of them is an amnesiac. Each conversation starts from zero, each preference explained again. We call it the amnesia tax, and every traveler pays it. This paper makes the case for the missing layer: a portable, human readable memory the traveler owns and grants to any agent, app, or service they choose. Traveler.md is who you are. Trip.md is what you're planning. The connected trip isn't a journey assembled inside one platform. It's a trip that stays connected to the person.

"We believe a traveler's memory should travel with them."

Help one billion travelers by building the founding travel AI infrastructure: owned by the traveler, governed for the enterprise, trusted across the network, and running on real travel.

The Third Voice told us where the market was going and why alignment would decide who deserves to hold it. Traveler.md is what we give the traveler so the agentic age works in their favor, not at their expense. And the network underneath is what makes all of it real instead of theoretical.

Why we acquired Sonder

The name is the thesis.

Sonder is the realization that every passing stranger has an inner life as vivid and complex as your own. It's the most precise word in travel for what we're building: a company that treats every traveler's tastes, history, and memories as singular, worth remembering, and worth carrying from one trip to the next. We didn't acquire a portfolio of buildings. We acquired the one brand in travel whose name already means what we mean.

Curated stays, chosen for taste.

Sonder is the most visible expression of the thesis these papers describe, and a clear example of how our brands work. Each brand gives us two of the four things an agentic network needs: distribution to high intent travelers, and a proving ground where memory, governance, and trust meet real bookings.

Sonder relaunches at Sonder.com as a curated guide to the world's best urban stays: design led apartment style and boutique accommodations, chosen for taste rather than served as infinite, undifferentiated choice. Stays are fulfilled through our distribution partners. It's a continuation, not a teardown. We're keeping what made Sonder Sonder, and moving its technology from the lock on the door to discovery, memory, and personalization.

A path we know, and where memory becomes real.

This is a path we know. We've acquired and revitalized Casai, Smartours, and OwnerDirect by pairing trusted travel names with modern distribution and applied AI. Sonder joins a portfolio we already know how to build, launch, and grow.

And it's where the memory thesis becomes real for travelers. Traveler.md, our traveler owned memory file, is in public beta and already being used to make trips more personal. Over time, we intend to make Sonder the flagship showcase for personalization led by memory: an experience designed to help the right traveler find the right place, and to remember them, with their permission, when they return. Our principle is simple. We never tell a traveler something they didn't share with us first.
